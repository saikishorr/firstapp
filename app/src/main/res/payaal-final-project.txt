it is usually not the preferred business or engineering decision to put everything into one application. 
the main reasons are.

| Reason                                   | Why it matters                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Different domains require deep expertise | Fraud detection, regulatory reporting, workflow, and data governance are each large product categories with dedicated teams and years of R&D. |
| Regulations change frequently            | Regulatory reporting must adapt quickly to new rules. Keeping it separate allows faster updates without affecting the rest of the system.     |
| Different users                          | Risk analysts, auditors, compliance officers, data engineers, and operations teams all have different workflows and interfaces.               |
| Scalability                              | Transaction ingestion may handle millions of events per second, while audit reporting is batch-oriented and doesn't need the same scale.      |
| Reliability                              | If one module fails (for example, reporting), banks don't want payment processing to stop.                                                    |
| Existing investments                     | Large banks have already spent millions integrating established enterprise products, making replacement costly and risky.                     |
| Vendor specialization                    | Specialized vendors often deliver stronger capabilities in their niche than a single general-purpose platform.                                |

final assessment

If I had to choose one direction to spend the next 2–3 years on, I would choose the **combined vision**, but with one important refinement:
Don't market it as a collection of modules.
Market it as a **decision-centric operating layer** for regulated enterprises.
The modules (risk, workflow, reporting, lineage, audit, explainability) become capabilities of that operating layer—not the product itself.
The one area where I'd invest the most design effort is the **Decision Trace Graph Framework**. If you can define a compelling graph model, demonstrate why it outperforms traditional logs and disconnected audit trails, and show practical benefits for investigations, compliance, and AI governance, that's where your strongest innovation is likely to emerge.
